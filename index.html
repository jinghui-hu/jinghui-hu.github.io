Human–Computer Interaction

About me

I study how we perceive, move, and interact—and how this can shape more human-centred technology.

I’m a Leverhulme Early Career Fellow in the School of Computing and Communications at Lancaster University, working with Hans Gellersen. My research connects models of human behaviour with the design of interactive systems, particularly gaze interaction, extended reality (XR), and human-centred AI.

I completed my PhD in Engineering at the University of Cambridge, supervised by Per Ola Kristensson. Before that, I studied Human–Computer Interaction at UCL and Computer Science and Technology at Zhejiang University of Technology.

Current research · Leverhulme Early Career Fellowship

Body-in-the-World Intelligence

Grounding AI in Human Sensorimotor Intelligence.

Research interests

  • Human perception and movement. Modelling eye–head–hand coordination and individual differences in interactive systems.
  • Interaction in extended reality. Designing multimodal techniques for text entry, selection, navigation, and everyday work in XR.
  • Embodied, human-centred AI. Connecting sensorimotor behaviour with intelligent systems that adapt to people.
